# Purpose

Define accessibility, inclusion, privacy comprehension, and trust as constitutional experience outcomes.

# Accessibility Invariants

Every workflow is keyboard operable, screen-reader understandable, zoom resilient, contrast sufficient, focus visible, motion optional, error-associated, and complete without pointer precision, color alone, audio alone, or time pressure without accommodation.

# Inclusive Interaction

Language is respectful, direct, age-appropriate, and free of assumptions about ability, family, finances, education, identity, or access. Equivalent paths support different literacy, connectivity, device, and assistive-technology contexts.

# Authentication Accessibility

Authentication, recovery, verification, timeout, and challenge mechanisms provide accessible alternatives and do not impose cognitive tests without accommodation. Security cannot rely on inaccessible interaction.

# Media and Documents

Media requires captions, transcripts or equivalent alternatives where applicable. Documents and exports preserve semantic structure, reading order, labels, language, contrast, and assistive-technology access.

# Privacy Comprehension

Before collection or sharing, users understand purpose, audience, required versus optional data, retention, revocation, automation, and consequences. Consent is specific, accessible, and not bundled with unrelated participation.

# Trust Invariants

Playbook never misrepresents verification, intelligence, progress, availability, delivery, authorship, or committed state. Users can identify automated assistance and the accountable application or person.

# Evidence

Certification requires automated testing, keyboard review, assistive-technology review, responsive and zoom review, contrast and motion checks, accessible authentication, document/media review, and representative user evaluation.

# Validation

PBOS shall fail accessibility certification when any required outcome lacks evidence, even when a visual or automated check passes.
