# Purpose

The Color System establishes the constitutional meaning and governance of color throughout the Playbook ecosystem.

Color communicates hierarchy, status, interaction, opportunity, trust, and feedback.

Color shall enhance understanding.

Color shall never become decoration without purpose.

---

# Constitutional Authority

This document derives authority from:

- PDS-000 — Playbook Design Philosophy
- PDS-001 — Visual Language Standards
- PDS-002 — Design Tokens
- PDS-004 — Typography System

All visual implementations shall inherit these standards.

---

# Guiding Principles

Color shall be:

- Intentional
- Accessible
- Semantic
- Consistent
- Inclusive
- Theme-aware
- Platform-independent

---

# Constitutional Philosophy

Users should understand meaning before recognizing branding.

Color exists to improve comprehension.

Meaning shall remain consistent regardless of implementation.

---

# Semantic Color Categories

Playbook recognizes the following constitutional color groups.

## Brand

Represents the Playbook identity.

Examples:

- Primary Brand
- Secondary Brand
- Accent Brand

Brand colors establish recognition.

They shall not replace functional meaning.

---

## Surface

Surface colors establish structure.

Examples include:

- Application Background
- Primary Surface
- Secondary Surface
- Elevated Surface
- Modal Surface
- Sidebar Surface

Surface colors communicate spatial relationships.

---

## Text

Text colors communicate emphasis.

Examples:

- Primary Text
- Secondary Text
- Tertiary Text
- Disabled Text
- Inverse Text

Text colors shall prioritize readability.

---

## Border

Border colors communicate:

- separation
- focus
- interaction
- grouping

Borders should reinforce—not replace—layout.

---

## Interactive

Interactive colors identify controls.

Examples:

- Primary Action
- Secondary Action
- Hover
- Active
- Focus
- Disabled

Interactive colors shall remain predictable throughout the platform.

---

# Status Colors

Status colors communicate application state.

## Success

Indicates:

- completion
- achievement
- validation
- approval

Success shall communicate confidence.

---

## Warning

Indicates:

- attention
- caution
- incomplete work
- potential issues

Warnings should encourage corrective action.

---

## Error

Indicates:

- failure
- validation errors
- blocked workflows
- security issues

Errors should clearly communicate recovery paths.

---

## Information

Indicates:

- guidance
- announcements
- educational content
- neutral updates

Information should never be confused with warnings.

---

# Opportunity Colors

Playbook introduces semantic opportunity colors.

These communicate growth rather than system status.

Examples include:

- Scholarship Opportunities
- Career Opportunities
- Mentorship Opportunities
- Internship Opportunities
- Financial Opportunities
- Volunteer Opportunities
- Leadership Opportunities
- Athletic Opportunities

Opportunity colors reinforce the Playbook mission.

---

# Progress Colors

Progress indicators shall communicate:

- not started
- in progress
- nearly complete
- completed

Progress should remain understandable without relying solely on color.

---

# AI Colors

AI-generated experiences shall remain visually distinguishable when appropriate.

AI recommendations should reinforce transparency rather than imply certainty.

---

# Notification Colors

Notification colors communicate urgency.

Priority examples:

- Critical
- High
- Normal
- Low

Priority should remain understandable using multiple visual signals.

---

# Data Visualization

Charts shall use governed semantic palettes.

Visualizations shall support:

- accessibility
- color-blind friendliness
- printing
- dark mode
- high contrast

Meaning shall remain consistent across all chart types.

---

# Themes

Every semantic color shall support:

- Light Theme
- Dark Theme
- High Contrast Theme

Future themes shall inherit semantic meaning.

Themes may redefine values.

Themes shall not redefine purpose.

---

# Accessibility

Color shall never be the sole indicator of:

- status
- validation
- selection
- interaction
- required fields
- errors
- opportunities

Interfaces shall provide redundant communication through:

- icons
- typography
- labels
- patterns
- motion (when appropriate)

---

# Contrast

All color combinations shall satisfy constitutional accessibility requirements.

Contrast shall be validated automatically whenever possible.

---

# Branding

Marketing experiences may emphasize brand identity.

Operational experiences shall prioritize usability.

Branding shall never reduce readability.

---

# International Considerations

Color meaning may vary across cultures.

Playbook implementations should consider regional interpretation while preserving constitutional intent.

---

# Future Expansion

Additional semantic categories may include:

- Healthcare
- Government
- Military
- Enterprise
- Research
- International Programs

Expansion shall preserve backwards compatibility.

---

# Design Tokens

Engineering implementations shall consume semantic color tokens rather than implementation-specific values.

Constitutional documents define meaning.

Implementation systems define exact values.

---

# PBOS Responsibilities

PBOS shall validate:

- approved semantic color usage
- accessibility compliance
- contrast requirements
- prohibited hard-coded values
- deprecated token usage
- theme compatibility
- visualization consistency

PBOS shall reject implementations violating constitutional color standards.

---

# Engineering Responsibilities

Engineering shall implement semantic colors using approved Design Tokens.

Direct implementation values shall remain outside constitutional governance.

---

# Governance

Changes to semantic meaning require constitutional review.

Visual refreshes may update implementation values without changing constitutional intent.

Breaking changes to semantic meaning require version updates.

---

# Success Criteria

Every Playbook interface communicates hierarchy, interaction, opportunity, progress, and trust through a governed semantic Color System.

Users should immediately understand the meaning of color regardless of device, operating system, theme, language, or future implementation technology.

The Color System serves as the constitutional authority for every use of color throughout the Playbook ecosystem.
