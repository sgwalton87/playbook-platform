# Purpose

PPS-3100 is the constitutional authority governing every Role Operating System in the Playbook Platform. It defines the common contract that role-specific documents inherit and the rules PBOS and contributors shall enforce.

# Scope

This specification governs role-oriented dashboards, navigation, applications, capabilities, workflows, intelligence, data access, permissions, relationships, communications, notifications, analytics, accessibility, security, lifecycle, and evolution.

It does not define product artifacts owned by Volume 30, canonical data owned by data architecture, or implementation details owned by future application and engineering specifications.

# Authority

PPS-3100 inherits PPS-000 through PPS-015, PPS-004, PPS-500, and Volume 30. Child Role Operating System specifications may narrow this contract but shall not weaken it.

# Constitutional Principles

Every Role Operating System shall:

- Advance the mission of an Operating System for Scholars.
- Preserve the Scholar Record as canonical learner evidence.
- Compose shared platform capabilities rather than duplicate them.
- Enforce least privilege and relationship-aware access.
- Remain interoperable with other authorized Operating Systems.
- Preserve human oversight for consequential intelligence.
- Meet the unified design language and accessibility standard.
- Produce auditable events, metrics, and lifecycle evidence.

# Definition of a Role Operating System

A Role Operating System is a governed composition of platform artifacts that enables a defined user population to fulfill authorized responsibilities and achieve measurable outcomes.

A Role Operating System is not:

- A standalone application.
- A database ownership boundary.
- A replacement for authorization policy.
- A fork of shared product capability.
- A role label without workflows and responsibilities.

# Standard Operating System Contract

Each Role Operating System shall declare:

1. Canonical identifier, title, owner, version, and lifecycle status.
2. Parent, upstream dependencies, related documents, and relationships.
3. Primary users, stakeholders, responsibilities, goals, and success definition.
4. Dashboard, navigation, applications, capabilities, and workflows.
5. Intelligence engines and human-oversight boundaries.
6. Data ownership, permissions, visibility, and audit requirements.
7. Cross-OS relationships, communications, and notifications.
8. Analytics, KPIs, accessibility, device experiences, and security.
9. Future evolution constraints and unresolved refinement requirements.

# Required Sections

Every child Role Operating System specification shall contain the section structure defined by the Volume 31 README and shall provide role-specific constitutional framing in every section. Empty headings are invalid.

# Operating System Lifecycle

Role Operating System specifications use the document lifecycle defined by PPS-008:

Draft → Review → Approved → Canonical → Deprecated → Archived

Implementation readiness is independent from document status and shall be recorded through governed implementation evidence. PPS-3195 defines detailed lifecycle controls.

# Cross-Operating System Collaboration

Cross-OS collaboration shall be relationship-aware, purpose-limited, consent-aware where required, and auditable. One Operating System shall not directly assume another role's permissions.

Shared work shall use registered workflows, events, communications, and notifications. PPS-3190 and PPS-3191 govern collaboration and communication contracts.

# Capability Reuse

Role Operating Systems shall reference registered Volume 30 capabilities. They shall not fork a feature, component, service, API, workflow, or data object solely to alter role presentation.

Role-specific differences shall be expressed through configuration, permissions, policy, composition, or an approved extension contract.

# Shared Intelligence Architecture

Intelligence capabilities shall inherit PPS-005, PPS-006, PPS-1200 through PPS-1209, and PPS-3012. Recommendations shall be explainable, evidence-based, observable, and subject to appropriate human review.

No Role Operating System may use intelligence to bypass permissions, make an unreviewable consequential decision, or mutate canonical records without authorized workflow controls.

# Security

Authentication establishes identity; authorization establishes permitted action. Every Role Operating System shall:

- Apply least privilege.
- Enforce server and data-layer policy.
- Respect record ownership and relationship grants.
- Define visibility and sharing boundaries.
- Audit privileged and consequential operations.
- Fail closed when identity, permission, or context is uncertain.

# Governance

Material changes require dependency review, identifier validation, security review, cross-OS impact analysis, and documentation updates. Constitutional conflicts follow PPS-015.

PBOS shall reject a Role Operating System specification that omits required metadata, sections, dependencies, permissions, or traceability.

# Accessibility

Every Role Operating System shall conform to PPS-013 and applicable accessibility standards. Equivalent outcomes shall be available across keyboard, assistive technology, reduced-motion, high-contrast, mobile, and responsive desktop contexts.

# Evolution Rules

Evolution shall preserve identifiers and compatibility unless an approved migration states otherwise. Deprecation requires a successor or explicit retirement rationale, migration plan, communication plan, and historical preservation.

New roles shall demonstrate that their responsibilities cannot be represented through an existing Operating System plus relationship and permission configuration.

# Relationship to Platform Architecture

Role Operating Systems consume identity, the Scholar Record, shared experiences, data services, event infrastructure, notifications, integrations, and intelligence. They do not own those constitutional domains.

# Relationship to Product Architecture

Volume 30 defines product artifact registries. Volume 31 binds registered artifacts to role responsibilities and permission contexts. A child specification shall reference Volume 30 identifiers rather than invent product architecture.

# Relationship to Future Platform Application Architecture

Future application architecture may map Role Operating System contracts to routes, modules, services, deployment units, and runtime composition. It shall preserve the authority, permissions, reuse requirements, and cross-OS contracts established here.

# PBOS Responsibilities

PBOS shall:

- Validate identifiers, numbering, metadata, dependencies, and required sections.
- Verify inheritance from PPS-3100.
- Detect unregistered product artifacts and duplicated capabilities.
- Check permission, security, accessibility, analytics, and observability declarations.
- Build bidirectional dependency and relationship graphs.
- Prevent implementation authorization when constitutional dependencies are unresolved.
- Preserve validation and migration evidence.

# Codex Responsibilities

Codex shall:

- Read PPS-3100 and applicable child documents before planning implementation.
- Resolve every proposed artifact to a canonical registry identifier.
- Preserve data ownership and permission boundaries.
- Identify unresolved skeleton content rather than invent details.
- Produce implementation, testing, documentation, and migration plans traceable to this volume.

# Validation Rules

A Role Operating System is structurally valid only when:

- Its identifier is unique and within the assigned range.
- Required metadata parses successfully.
- Its parent is PPS-3100.
- All dependencies resolve.
- Every required section contains substantive framing.
- Product artifacts use registered identifiers.
- Permissions do not exceed upstream authority.
- Cross-OS interactions declare relationship and audit controls.
- Analytics and security requirements are testable.

# Definition of Done

PPS-3100 is complete when the Role Operating System contract, inheritance rules, lifecycle, collaboration model, reuse requirements, intelligence safeguards, security, accessibility, governance, evolution, PBOS enforcement, and implementation boundaries are explicit and registered.
