# Volume 35 AI Governance Model

## Purpose

Govern AI-generated experiences, components, personalization, recommendations, adaptations, and automated changes so autonomous systems cannot acquire constitutional authority or bypass human accountability.

This model inherits from the canonical Playbook AI authority when established. If that authority cannot be resolved, AI-generated Volume 35 changes are blocked.

## Scope

This model governs AI-generated components, layouts, patterns, workflows, recommendations, personalization, accessibility adaptation, automated modification, validation assistance, monitoring, suspension, rollback, and historical accountability.

## Authority

Playbook AI Governance supplies higher-order AI authority. Volume 35 AI Experience Authority governs experience-specific policy. Accountable human/organization owners approve outcomes. AI systems have no approval, certification, amendment, exception, or lifecycle authority.

## Ownership

Every AI action names a human or organization Business Owner, Experience Architecture Owner, Technical Owner, Steward, Validator, and Certifier. The model/provider owns its service obligations and provenance evidence but never owns constitutional truth.

## Artifact Model

An AI-governed artifact contains the generated experience artifact, generation manifest, model/agent/tool identities, authorized inputs, policy envelope, provenance, validation evidence, human approval, certification, monitoring plan, rollback target, and immutable event history.

## Constitutional Principle

AI is a producer or advisor, never a constitutional authority.

Every AI-generated artifact has an accountable human or organization owner. Model output, confidence, popularity, or observed engagement cannot substitute for approval, validation, certification, or lifecycle authority.

## Governed AI Actions

| Action | Required Authority | Maximum Default Effect |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Generate component or pattern proposal | scoped AI generation grant plus accountable owner | `PROPOSED` artifact only |
| Generate workflow/experience composition | application/experience owner | noncanonical proposal or certified bounded runtime variant |
| Personalize presentation | user/organization policy and consent | within certified variability envelope |
| Recommend content or action | recommendation authority | informational recommendation unless separately authorized |
| Adapt accessibility | accessibility authority and user preference | must preserve or improve certified access |
| Modify canonical artifact | constitutional amendment authority | prohibited without full human-governed lifecycle |
| Activate extension | extension and organization authorities | prohibited based on AI decision alone |

## AI-Generated Artifact Ownership

The accountable Owner:

- defines purpose and permitted scope;
- accepts responsibility for the generated artifact;
- confirms source/data authority;
- supplies required evidence;
- responds to findings and incidents;
- cannot delegate accountability to the model provider.

The generating agent identity and model provider remain evidence producers, not owners.

## AI Generation Manifest

Every governed generation records:

- run and correlation IDs;
- accountable owner and organization;
- agent, model, provider, version, tool, and policy identities;
- input/source identities and authorization;
- prompt/procedure/configuration digest where retention policy permits;
- requested artifact and allowed scope;
- constitutional/rule versions;
- output artifact ID/version/digest;
- confidence/uncertainty information when meaningful;
- data classifications, consent, privacy, and retention;
- validation results;
- human review/approval;
- activation, monitoring, rollback, and expiry.

Sensitive inputs must not be preserved in evidence beyond authorized policy. Required provenance must remain independently verifiable.

## Protected Invariants

AI may not:

- invent authority, identity, evidence, dependency, approval, or lifecycle state;
- weaken accessibility, privacy, security, tenant isolation, or user control;
- redefine canonical semantics;
- conceal generated origin;
- create deceptive or manipulative interaction;
- make a high-impact or irreversible decision without required human authority;
- expand permissions or data access;
- self-certify or approve its own output;
- silently learn from or share tenant data outside authorized scope;
- bypass extension isolation or runtime governance.

## Governance Rules

- AI output begins as a proposal unless a pre-certified automation envelope explicitly permits bounded operational variation.
- Human approval is mandatory for constitutional, shared, high-impact, sensitive, or cross-organization change.
- Personalization remains within certified semantic and permission boundaries.
- Generated origin and accountable ownership remain transparent.
- Models cannot self-validate, self-certify, or expand their authority.
- Drift, unsafe behavior, or unverifiable provenance triggers suspension.

## Personalization And Adaptation

Personalization requires:

- declared user benefit and lawful purpose;
- user/organization policy and consent where required;
- certified variability boundaries;
- semantic and workflow invariants;
- accessibility equivalence;
- explanation and control appropriate to impact;
- safe default and opt-out where policy requires;
- bias, fairness, privacy, and performance evidence;
- continuous monitoring and deterministic rollback.

Personalization cannot change constitutional meaning, permission, obligation, or safety based solely on inferred traits.

## Human Approval Requirements

Human approval is mandatory for:

- canonical or shared-platform changes;
- new component/pattern semantics;
- breaking or high-impact changes;
- permission, identity, privacy, security, financial, education, employment, accessibility, or safety effects;
- cross-organization or partner distribution;
- unresolved uncertainty or material validator disagreement.

Approval binds the exact output digest. Editing or regeneration invalidates approval.

## AI Validation

PBOS AI Experience Validator verifies:

- owner/agent/model identity;
- generation scope and authority;
- provenance and content digest;
- constitutional metadata;
- component, accessibility, dependency, inheritance, state, compatibility, extension, privacy, security, and performance rules;
- protected invariants;
- human approval;
- monitoring/recovery readiness.

AI may assist validation but cannot be the sole validator for a rule requiring independent or human evidence.

## Validation Model

PBOS evaluates authority, ownership, provenance, inputs, policy scope, protected invariants, component/layout rules, accessibility, privacy/security, fairness, compatibility, tenant isolation, human approval, certification, monitoring, and rollback readiness.

## Automated Changes

Automated change is permitted only inside a pre-certified policy envelope defining:

- allowed artifacts and fields;
- invariant rules;
- maximum impact;
- frequency/rate limits;
- evidence and monitoring;
- rollback trigger;
- expiry;
- accountable owner.

Changes outside the envelope remain proposals.

## Lifecycle And Certification

AI-generated artifacts use the same lifecycle and certification model as human-produced artifacts. Generated origin is permanent provenance. A model/version/policy change triggers revalidation when it can affect certified behavior.

## Lifecycle Management

Generated artifacts traverse the canonical artifact lifecycle. Model, prompt/procedure, tool, policy, input authority, or material output changes create a new revision and validation attempt. Operational suspension does not erase certification or lifecycle history.

## Monitoring And Evidence

Operational evidence includes:

- behavior and outcome measures;
- accessibility and error monitoring;
- drift and policy violation detection;
- user complaints and appeals;
- security/privacy incidents;
- override, suspension, rollback, and revocation;
- affected artifact/organization identities.

Engagement alone is not proof of constitutional compliance.

## Evidence Requirements

Evidence includes accountable owner, organization, agent/model/provider/tool versions, authorized sources, generation procedure digest, output identity, constitutional/rule versions, validation, human approval, consent/privacy, fairness/accessibility, activation, telemetry, drift, incidents, rollback, suspension, and revocation.

## Failure Behavior

Unknown authority, missing provenance, invalid scope, failed validation, absent human approval, drift, unsafe behavior, or evidence mismatch blocks generation use or triggers suspension.

PBOS preserves current canonical truth, quarantines the output, records evidence, identifies affected dependents, and requires governed review before restoration.

## Ownership

AI Experience Authority owns policy. Accountable human/organization Owner owns the proposed outcome. AI Governance Steward preserves records. Validators own results. Certification Authority owns certification. Lifecycle Authority owns promotion. The AI system owns none of these decisions.

## Future Evolution

Future models, agents, modalities, and autonomous techniques may operate only through compatible authority and evidence envelopes. Increased capability never increases constitutional authority automatically.
