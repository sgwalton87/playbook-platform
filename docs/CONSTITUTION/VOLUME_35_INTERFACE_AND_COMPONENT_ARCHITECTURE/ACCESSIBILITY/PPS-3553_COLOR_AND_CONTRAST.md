# Purpose

Establish the constitutional architecture governing color usage and visual contrast throughout the Playbook Platform.

Color shall enhance comprehension, accessibility, and usability without becoming the sole method of communicating meaning.

---

# Mission

Provide a unified Color and Contrast Architecture that guarantees accessible visual communication across every Playbook application, operating system, and device.

---

# Scope

This document governs:

- Text contrast
- Background contrast
- Interactive controls
- Icons
- Charts
- Status indicators
- Focus indicators
- Color palettes
- Dark mode
- High-contrast experiences

---

# Constitutional Principles

## Accessibility First

Color combinations shall satisfy constitutional accessibility requirements before aesthetic preferences.

---

## Meaning Beyond Color

Color shall never be the sole indicator of status, meaning, or required action.

---

## Consistency

Equivalent semantic meanings shall use equivalent color systems throughout the platform.

---

## Readability

Visual contrast shall maximize readability under varying environmental conditions.

---

## Adaptability

Interfaces shall support multiple viewing environments while preserving usability.

---

# Constitutional Color Categories

Playbook recognizes:

- Primary
- Secondary
- Success
- Warning
- Error
- Informational
- Neutral
- Focus
- Disabled
- Background

---

# PBOS Responsibilities

PBOS shall validate:

- Contrast compliance
- Semantic consistency
- Design Token inheritance
- Cross-platform compatibility
- Accessibility requirements

---

# Governance

Applications shall inherit constitutional Color and Contrast Architecture.

Interfaces failing constitutional contrast requirements shall not achieve PBOS certification.

# Relationship to Other Documents

This document supports:

# Future Evolution

Future constitutional amendments may introduce adaptive contrast systems, environmental lighting adaptation, personalized accessibility preferences, and emerging display technologies while preserving constitutional accessibility.
