# Purpose

Establish the constitutional architecture governing color throughout the Playbook Platform.

The Color System defines semantic meaning, accessibility requirements, visual hierarchy, and consistent communication across every interface.

---

# Mission

Provide a semantic, accessible, and scalable color architecture that enables every Playbook experience to communicate information consistently while supporting future evolution.

---

# Scope

This document governs:

- Brand colors
- Surface colors
- Background colors
- Text colors
- Interactive colors
- Feedback colors
- Status colors
- Border colors
- Theme architecture
- Accessibility contrast

---

# Constitutional Principles

## Meaning Before Appearance

Color communicates meaning before aesthetics.

Semantic intent shall always take precedence over stylistic preference.

---

## Semantic Architecture

Every constitutional color shall represent a defined semantic purpose.

Examples include:

- Primary
- Secondary
- Success
- Warning
- Error
- Information
- Disabled
- Selected
- Hover
- Focus

---

## Accessibility

Color shall never serve as the sole indicator of meaning.

Equivalent non-color indicators shall exist wherever required.

---

## Consistency

Equivalent meanings shall use equivalent semantic colors throughout the platform.

Independent application color systems are constitutionally prohibited.

---

## Theme Independence

Semantic meaning shall remain constant regardless of presentation theme.

Supported themes include:

- Light
- Dark
- High Contrast

Additional themes may be introduced through constitutional amendment.

# PBOS Responsibilities

PBOS shall validate:

- Semantic consistency
- Accessibility compliance
- Theme compatibility
- Component inheritance

# Governance

All constitutional color definitions shall inherit from the Design Token Architecture.

Applications and components shall consume constitutional color definitions rather than defining independent palettes.
