# Purpose

Establish the constitutional architecture governing observability throughout execution.

Execution that cannot be observed cannot be constitutionally governed.

Every execution shall produce sufficient evidence to understand what occurred, why it occurred, who authorized it, and whether constitutional requirements were satisfied.

---

# Scope

Applies to:

- Commands
- Workflows
- Events
- Automations
- Background jobs
- AI-assisted execution
- Administrative execution
- Distributed execution

---

# Constitutional Principles

Execution observability shall be:

- Complete
- Deterministic
- Immutable
- Traceable
- Auditable
- Explainable
- Governed

---

# Observability Model

Every execution shall produce observable evidence including:

- Constitutional identity
- Execution identity
- Actor identity
- Authorization reference
- Context reference
- Lifecycle transitions
- State transitions
- Timestamp history
- Outcome
- Failure information
- Recovery activity

---

# Evidence Requirements

Observable evidence shall:

- Remain immutable
- Preserve provenance
- Support replay
- Support auditing
- Support governance review

Historical evidence shall never be rewritten.

---

# Monitoring

Execution monitoring shall detect:

- Failures
- Delays
- Policy violations
- Unauthorized execution attempts
- Recovery operations
- Eligibility, execution, outcome, and evidence certification failures

Monitoring shall inform governance without altering constitutional truth.

---

# Explainability

Every execution shall remain explainable.

Constitutional stakeholders shall be able to determine:

- What happened
- Why it happened
- Who initiated it
- What constitutional rules applied
- What outcome resulted

---

# Governance

Observability exists to preserve trust, accountability, transparency, and constitutional integrity.
