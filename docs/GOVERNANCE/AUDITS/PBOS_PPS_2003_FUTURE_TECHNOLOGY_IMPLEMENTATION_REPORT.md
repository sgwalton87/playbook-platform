## Constitutional Review

PPS-2003 was reviewed against PPS-000, PPS-2000, PPS-2002, PPS-2004, PPS-900, PPS-1100, PPS-1200, PPS-2008, and PPS-2009. The review found that its original text stated evaluation, security, data, AI, and constitutional alignment at a high level but did not define a mandatory end-to-end technology lifecycle, continuous review, registry requirements, vendor neutrality, interoperability, or explicit human accountability.

No contradictory authority was introduced. PPS-2009 remains the final constitutional checkpoint; PPS-2003 defines the technology-specific lifecycle underneath that authority. PPS-2004 continues to inherit from PPS-2003 for artificial intelligence evolution. Related links, rather than new dependency edges, connect PPS-2003 to PPS-900, PPS-1100, PPS-1200, PPS-2008, and PPS-2009, avoiding circular dependency creation.

## Constitutional Improvements

- Added the mandatory lifecycle: Discovery, Evaluation, Pilot, Governance Approval, Limited Deployment, Platform Integration, Ongoing Review, and Retirement.
- Established that no technology may bypass constitutional lifecycle governance.
- Made technology permanently subordinate to constitutional protections, human agency, governance authority, privacy, security, and constitutional rights.
- Established that approval is never permanent and may lead to modification, restriction, suspension, or retirement.
- Required vendor neutrality, portability, and constitutional independence from providers and manufacturers.
- Preserved human judgment and accountability.
- Required interoperability without weakening governance boundaries.
- Established permanent, versioned, auditable Constitutional Technology Registry requirements.
- Established ethical innovation priorities and prohibited efficiency or convenience from overriding constitutional principles.
- Strengthened validation, compliance, and Definition of Done requirements.
