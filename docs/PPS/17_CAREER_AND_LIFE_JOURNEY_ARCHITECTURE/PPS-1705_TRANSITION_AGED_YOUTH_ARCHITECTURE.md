# Purpose

Establish the constitutional architecture supporting young people navigating major life transitions, including education, employment, independence, and identity development.

---

# Scope

Applies to:

- Young adults
- First-generation college students
- Career entrants
- Opportunity seekers
- Returning learners
- Emerging leaders

---

# Authority

Transitions should be supported as developmental journeys rather than viewed only as moments of risk.

---

# Constitutional Principles

## Support During Change

Transitions require guidance, resources, and relationships.

---

## Independence Through Preparation

Young people should develop the skills necessary for self-direction.

---

## Belonging Matters

Community support improves successful transitions.

---

## Multiple Pathways Exist

Success may include:

- College
- Career
- Entrepreneurship
- Service
- Skilled trades
- Continuing education

---

# Transition Architecture

Supports:

- Goal setting
- Career exploration
- Financial preparation
- Mentorship
- Skill development
- Opportunity discovery
- Community connection

---

# Responsibilities

The transition system shall:

- Identify upcoming transitions.
- Provide resources.
- Connect support networks.
- Encourage confidence.
- Track progress.

---

# Validation Rules

Systems shall:

- Respect user autonomy.
- Avoid assumptions.
- Protect sensitive information.
- Provide explainable guidance.

---

# Compliance Requirements

Integrates with:

- Learning Architecture
- Opportunity Architecture
- Community Architecture
- Financial Empowerment Architecture

---

# Implementation Guidance

Transition support should focus on empowerment rather than dependency.

---

# Definition of Done

Transition-aged users have access to the resources, relationships, and pathways necessary for successful next steps.

---

# Future Amendments

Future versions may include:

- Transition forecasting
- AI transition coaching
- Regional resource networks
- Adult learner pathways
